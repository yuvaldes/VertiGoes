In an emergency
If you experience a sudden severe headache, diﬃculty speaking, facial drooping, one sided

The VertiGoes app exists to accompany people living with dizziness, vertigo and balance disorders: to
explain, to oﬀer tools for the hard moments, to let you keep an orderly record of what is happening to
you, and to help you arrive prepared for a conversation with a professional. That is what it sets out to do,

2. Manoeuvres and exercises, the most important section here
The app presents therapeutic manoeuvres and vestibular rehabilitation exercises. It is important that you
understand their central limitation: whether a manoeuvre works depends on it being performed on the
correct ear and the correct canal, and identifying that is done through a clinical examination by a
professional, not in an app and not by you alone. A manoeuvre performed on the wrong side may fail to
help, and can in some cases move crystals into another canal and make things worse.
So: talk to a physician or a vestibular physiotherapist before you start, and especially if you have
problems with your neck, your heart, your blood vessels, your eyes or your balance, if you have had
surgery on your head, neck, back or eyes, or if you are pregnant. That consultation is not a formality, it is
what turns an exercise from a guess into treatment.

The exercises involve rapid movements of the head and body, and by their nature may cause dizziness,

at home. Do not drive, operate machinery or climb stairs soon after an exercise, and give yourself time to
settle.
Stop immediately if you develop sharp pain, neck or back pain, an abnormal heartbeat, chest pain,
shortness of breath, fainting or a feeling that you are about to faint, or any symptom that strikes you as

3. Warning signs that need a clinician, not an app
Dizziness and imbalance are symptoms, not a diagnosis, and in some cases they point to an urgent
medical condition such as a stroke, an intracranial bleed, an acute infection or a cardiac problem. Do not
assume that what you are feeling is BPPV simply because that is what the app is about. If your
symptoms are new, sudden, diﬀerent from your usual pattern, accompanied by any of the signs listed at
the top of this screen, or simply worrying you, get medical assessment before performing any exercise.

genuine emergency the right number is 101, not your contact.

Accessibility Statement
VertiGoes app · Last updated: 27 September 2026 · Version 1.0
Our commitment
This app was built for people living with dizziness, vertigo and balance disorders. Almost everyone who
uses it will sometimes do so at their worst moment: when the room is spinning, when focusing the eyes
is hard, when movement on screen makes the nausea worse, and when their hand is shaking. So
accessibility in this app is not a box to tick, it is the condition for it working at all.
We work to make it accessible in line with the Israeli Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law, 1998,
and the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Regulations (Accessibility Adjustments to Service),
2013.
The accessibility level, and where it stands today
Our target is the WCAG 2.1 level AA guidelines, alongside Apple's and Google's own accessibility
guidance.
We will say it plainly, because it is what we would want to be told: the app is in development and does
not yet meet level AA. It has not been reviewed by a certiﬁed accessibility professional and holds no
formal accessibility certiﬁcation. We intend to complete an external professional review and ﬁx what it
ﬁnds before wide release, and to update this statement accordingly.
Until then, if anything in the app blocks your way, we want to know, and we will make the adjustment you
need. Contact details are at the end of this statement.
What is already in the app
A quiet interface with no ﬂashing. There is no ﬂashing content, no video that plays by itself and no
continuous background animation. This is a deliberate design decision for people whose vestibular
symptoms are triggered by movement on a screen.
Respect for the reduce motion preference. The app carries handling for the operating system's
reduce motion setting. We are extending that cover to every transition in the app.
The display can be enlarged. The app does not block device zoom, so content can be made bigger.
Short screens and plain language. Each screen asks for one thing, the text is short, and the wording
avoids unnecessary medical jargon. This is an adjustment for people with diﬃculty concentrating,
cognitive fatigue or brain fog, all of them very common in the vestibular world.
A permanent emergency button. An emergency button sits on the main screen, one tap away at the
moment when searching is hard.

===== PAGE 22 =====
Known limitations, and what we are doing about them
These are the failures we are currently aware of. We would rather write them here than wait for you to
discover them:
Colour contrast. Some of the text in the app, mainly the small labels at the top of cards and
secondary text, does not meet the 4.5:1 contrast ratio that level AA requires. We are darkening those
shades.
Text size. Some text is too small for people who struggle to focus their eyes during an episode. We
are enlarging it and testing the app against extreme font enlargement in the device settings.
Structure for screen readers. The screens still lack an ordered heading structure and marked
content and navigation regions, so someone using a screen reader will ﬁnd them hard to navigate and
hard to start from. This is the most signiﬁcant failure we know of, and it is ﬁrst on our list.
Spoken descriptions for buttons. Some buttons are read by the screen reader as a run of words
with no clear separation. We are ﬁxing their labelling.
Touch target size. Some buttons are below the recommended minimum of 44 points, which makes
them harder to use with an unsteady hand. We are enlarging them.
Hebrew and right to left. The app is currently presented in English and left to right only. The Hebrew
version, including a full ﬂip of the layout and correct language markup for screen readers, is in
progress.
Dark mode. Dark mode is not ﬁnished. For some people with light sensitivity this is a meaningful
adjustment, and we are aware of that.
Found a problem? Tell us
If there was a screen, a button or a piece of content you could not use, we want to know. It helps us ﬁx it,
and it helps everyone who comes after you. To let us deal with it quickly, please tell us which screen it
was, what you were trying to do, which device and operating system you have, and, if you were using
assistive technology, which one.
